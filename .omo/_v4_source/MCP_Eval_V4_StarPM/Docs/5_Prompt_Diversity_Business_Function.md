# **Diverse Prompts Tips**

[**Star Property Management \- Task Categories**](https://static.remotasks.com/uploads/69a74e16c6538223558b70f6/03_StarPM_TASK%20CATEGORIES.html) — 5 categories and 16 subcategories, each with an authoring checklist (eligible personas, systems, artifacts, linked scenarios), a worked example in the protagonist's voice, and short explanations of the property-management concepts it touches.  
Star PM tasks live at the intersection of physical operations and calendar-driven timing. Real work here means coordinating a make-ready turn end-to-end (move-out → punch-list → vendor scheduling → QC → marketing-ready), threading a rent delinquency through its statutory notice ladder without missing a window, packaging a CapEx recommendation for an owner call, running the interactive process on a Fair Housing accommodation inside the 10-day window, or reconciling a vendor invoice against the specific units it claims to have serviced. The failure mode is shallow — a prompt that just says "update the Make-Ready record" or "email the tenant back" isn't a Star PM task, it's an inbox chore.

🎯 Use this guide to diversify by workflow shape. Star PM's five categories move at different tempos: property-level daily operations run on turnover cycles and tenant response; portfolio-tier work runs on the weekly ops sync and owner-report cadence; QC and field services get called into other people's cycles; maintenance is ticket-driven and vendor-heavy; leasing runs on pipeline-stage progression through HubSpot and the 60-day renewal window. Author across the range. A task where Brooke reconciles a landscape invoice with Teresa reads nothing like a task where Patricia files an eviction packet with the JP Court Clerk, and both should show up in your body of work.

⚡ Match the pattern, not just the example. The worked examples are illustrations of what a rich, multi-service, multi-actor task looks like — most touch four or five systems (Airtable \+ Slack \+ Linear \+ Gmail \+ QuickBooks is a common combination) and thread through three or four cast members before they close. Build your own tasks from the authoring-checklist anchors and the live universe data. The examples are the shape to hit, not words to copy.

[**StarPM \- Task Categories**](https://static.remotasks.com/uploads/69a74e16c6538223558b70f6/03_StarPM_TASK%20CATEGORIES.html) — 10 categories and 42 subcategories. Each subcategory carries an authoring checklist (eligible personas, entities, systems, artifacts, linked scenarios), a worked example in the protagonist’s voice, and inline definitions of every accounting concept it touches. Categories carry a 🎓 CPA-heavy or 🛠 No-CPA-required tag for pipeline routing — informational, not gating; the guide is written for a generalist audience.

* StarPM tasks should reflect real accounting and advisory work — close cycles, partner sign-offs, controls testing, AML threshold reviews, AP escalations, change-order disputes — not just “check the GL and send an email.” When you’re stuck for a starting point, pick a subcategory and build from its worked example.

* 🎯 **Use it to diversify**. If all your tasks look the same, come back here. Each category exercises different systems (QuickBooks vs. QuickBooks vs. Linear vs. Airtable), different entities (StarPM’s own books vs. Northstar’s lawyer-trust work vs. Acme’s SaaS close), and different kinds of agent reasoning (the matching principle, FinCEN wire-flag thresholds, OOS absorb-vs-change-order judgement, IOLTA-trust roll-forward, GAAP-improper-request refusal). Your tasks should span that range over time, not cluster on one pattern.

* ⚡ **Follow the patterns, not just the examples**. The worked examples in each subcategory are illustrations, not templates to copy. Use them to understand what a rich, multi-action task looks like in this domain — then build your own from the authoring-checklist anchors and what you find in the live universe data.
